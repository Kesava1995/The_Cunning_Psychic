Chapter 3: The Fire That Never Happened

The sprawling factory floor of Lokhandi Dhatu in Baner was a place where
absolute silence was a foreign concept. The relentless clang of heavy
metal against metal echoed through the massive workspace, while
forklifts methodically navigated their way across the brightly marked
industrial lanes. High-pressure compressed air hissed loudly through
overhead pipes, and the massive diesel engines and machinery rumbled
continuously like a distant, approaching thunderstorm. For the vast
majority of the employees working there, this overwhelming cacophony
simply faded into a dull, manageable background hum after their first
few weeks on the job. For Kusha, however, the noise never truly
disappeared. As a natural observer who absorbed his surroundings with
intense focus, he noticed absolutely everything.

He tracked the specific, repetitive rhythm of an aging machine, caught
the subtle hesitation in a junior worker\'s voice, and easily read the
tight expressions of floor supervisors who were desperately trying to
mask their mounting stress. For Kusha, these seemingly insignificant
details often revealed far more profound truths than the most obvious
facts.

That particular morning, the factory floor was bustling with an unusual
level of frantic energy. A brand new, highly anticipated industrial
cutting machine had finally been delivered after months of logistical
delays. This bulky piece of equipment was worth several lakhs, and its
arrival had everyone on edge. The old, worn-out machine it was scheduled
to replace had already been completely disconnected from the main power
supply and unceremoniously pushed to an isolated corner of the factory
floor to await final disposal.

Eager workers and a specialized installation team immediately surrounded
the newly delivered equipment, as everyone, especially Kusha\'s floor
supervisor, wanted the complicated installation process finished before
the end of the shift.

\"Good,\" the supervisor declared briskly, quickly double-checking his
clipboard of paperwork. \"Let\'s get this machine inside immediately\".

Several workers dutifully stepped forward to begin moving the heavy
load, but Kusha paused and looked intensely at the new equipment. He
then shifted his gaze toward the isolated old machine sitting quietly in
the corner. Suddenly, a profound sense that something was terribly wrong
washed over him. It was not a logical deduction or a physical
observation, but rather a distinct, familiar pressure slowly building
behind his eyes. Having experienced this unique sensation enough times
throughout his life, he immediately recognized it not as a clear visual
prophecy, but as a dire warning arriving just moments before the actual
evidence. He stood completely still in the middle of the floor for
several long seconds, prompting the workers to pause in confusion while
the supervisor\'s brow furrowed into a deep frown.

\"Kusha?\" the supervisor called out impatiently.

\"We should probably keep it outside for now,\" Kusha suggested
smoothly, his voice carrying its usual calm authority.

The supervisor blinked in sheer disbelief. \"What? You mean the new
machine?\".

\"Yes,\" Kusha replied.

\"We literally just received it after waiting for months,\" the
supervisor argued, his frustration visibly rising.

\"I know,\" Kusha nodded patiently.

\"And we have an entire installation team standing right here ready to
work,\" the supervisor added, gesturing to the waiting crew.

\"I know,\" Kusha repeated with maddening calmness.

The supervisor stared at him, bewildered. \"Then why on earth should we
keep it sitting outside?\".

Kusha silently considered his response for a moment. He knew he could
not tell them that moving the new equipment inside now would result in
catastrophic damage. He certainly could not explain that in a matter of
minutes, the discarded machine sitting in the corner was going to
violently catch fire, or that nobody would believe him until the flames
actually appeared.

Instead, utilizing his signature evasiveness, he simply said, \"Let\'s
just wait\".

The supervisor crossed his arms defensively over his chest. \"That is
absolutely not a valid explanation, Kusha\".

Kusha offered a gentle, reassuring smile. \"It will make perfect sense a
little later\".

Letting out a heavy groan of defeat, the supervisor realized that
arguing with Kusha was ultimately a pointless endeavor. He had managed
Kusha long enough to know that the young man possessed a highly
irritating habit of being right, even if nobody could ever figure out
exactly how he managed it.

\"Fine,\" the supervisor snapped finally. \"You have exactly ten
minutes\".

\"That is more than enough,\" Kusha agreed.

The factory workers, the delivery crew, and the specialized installation
team all exchanged thoroughly confused glances, completely baffled by
the delay. Kusha, remaining the only perfectly calm person in the
vicinity, casually strolled over to a group of resting workers.

\"Tea?\" he offered warmly.

Several heads immediately snapped in his direction. One of the older
workers let out a loud, bewildered laugh. \"Tea? Right now?\".

\"Yes,\" Kusha confirmed easily.

\"But we have a mountain of work to do,\" the man protested weakly.

\"We will still have plenty of work to do after we finish our tea,\"
Kusha countered logically.

The workers looked at each other, silently acknowledging the universal
truth that nobody in the history of manual labor had ever successfully
refused a cup of free tea. Within minutes, Kusha had smoothly guided the
vast majority of the nearby workers away from the loading zone and
toward the safety of the factory canteen.

Watching this bizarre exodus unfold, the supervisor shook his head in
sheer disbelief. First, the boy inexplicably halts a crucial machinery
installation, and now he is orchestrating an impromptu tea break.
Muttering sarcastically about how wonderful his morning was going, the
supervisor spun on his heel and marched toward his private office,
deciding that filling out mundane paperwork would be significantly less
frustrating than dealing with Kusha.

Exactly five minutes later, a frantic shout echoed sharply across the
sprawling factory floor. It was immediately followed by another panicked
yell, and then several more voices joined the chorus.

\"Fire!\".

Inside his office, the supervisor froze completely. His office chair
rolled backward violently as he scrambled to his feet, initially praying
that he had simply misheard the commotion. Then, the piercing wail of
the facility\'s emergency alarm confirmed his worst fears. With his
stomach dropping rapidly, he sprinted out of his office.

A crowd of workers was already forming a cautious perimeter. Thick,
black smoke was billowing aggressively from the isolated corner where
the old machine had been parked. Bright orange flames violently burst
from the side of the metal housing, likely sparked by a hidden
electrical fault or residual industrial oil, though the exact cause was
irrelevant at that moment. The only thing that mattered was that the
massive piece of equipment was fully engulfed in fire.

The supervisor\'s heart hammered against his ribs as he immediately
whipped his head toward the exact spot where the installation crew
should have been working. It was entirely empty. There were no workers,
no engineers, and thankfully, no expensive replacement machine sitting
in the danger zone. There was absolutely nothing in the area except for
the burning old equipment.

Within moments, emergency fire extinguishers were deployed, and the
facility\'s rapid response team successfully contained the blaze. The
immediate danger vanished almost as quickly as it had materialized,
leaving behind zero injuries, zero casualties, and absolutely no
property damage beyond the already condemned machine.

As the supervisor slowly exhaled a shaky breath of relief, a terrifying
realization slammed into him. If the heavy new machine had been moved
inside as he had originally commanded, it would have been sitting
directly beside the inferno. The entire installation team would have
been working intimately around it, and dozens of floor workers would
have been clustered in the immediate vicinity. The resulting tragedy
could have been utterly devastating. Naturally, his thoughts immediately
gravitated toward the one person responsible for averting the disaster:
Kusha.

By the time the evening shift rolled around, the factory had somehow
managed to return to its normal, chaotic rhythm. The charred remains of
the old machine were securely isolated once again, the brand-new
replacement equipment was finally installed without further incident,
and the company\'s strict production schedules miraculously remained
intact.

Kusha was quietly sitting at his workstation, meticulously reviewing a
clipboard of calibration data, when he noticed someone approaching his
desk. He looked up to find his supervisor standing there, but for the
first time all day, the older man did not look irritated or confused.
Instead, his expression was incredibly thoughtful and subdued.

\"Kusha,\" the supervisor began softly.

\"Sir,\" Kusha acknowledged, putting down his pen.

A heavy silence hung between them for a long moment before the
supervisor finally let out a tired sigh. \"I owe you a massive
apology\".

Kusha offered a slight, unassuming smile. \"For what?\".

\"For losing my temper and being angry with you this morning,\" the
supervisor admitted. \"You somehow knew that something terrible was
about to happen\".

Leaning back comfortably in his chair, Kusha offered his standard
deflection. \"I just had a feeling\".

\"A feeling,\" the supervisor repeated flatly.

\"Yes,\" Kusha confirmed.

The supervisor let out a sudden, barking laugh, not because he found the
situation particularly humorous, but because his brain simply had no
other logical response left to process the absurdity of it all. \"So, a
mere feeling is what made you completely halt a vital factory
installation?\".

\"Apparently so,\" Kusha agreed mildly.

\"And this same feeling is what miraculously compelled you to drag half
of my workforce away from the danger zone for a cup of tea?\" the
supervisor pressed.

\"To be fair, the tea was exceptionally good today,\" Kusha quipped.

The supervisor shook his head in absolute bewilderment. \"I do not
understand you at all, Kusha\".

\"You are certainly not alone in that regard,\" Kusha replied easily.

For the first time that entire chaotic day, both men shared a genuine,
tension-relieving laugh. The supervisor leaned forward and extended a
grateful hand. \"Thank you\".

Kusha accepted the handshake firmly. \"It was no problem at all\".

Before walking away, the supervisor hesitated, unable to resist asking
the one burning question that everyone who knew Kusha eventually asked.
\"But seriously, how did you actually know?\".

As Kusha released the older man\'s hand, he prepared to deliver the
exact same rehearsed answer he always relied upon. \"It was just a lucky
guess\".

The supervisor stared at him for a long, scrutinizing second before
dramatically rolling his eyes. \"You are absolutely impossible,\" he
muttered affectionately before turning and walking back toward his
office.

Kusha allowed a genuine smile to grace his features before turning his
attention back to his calibration data. To an outside observer, the day
had been entirely ordinary: an old machine was replaced, a minor fire
was swiftly extinguished, and daily production successfully resumed.
There was nothing noticeably extraordinary about the sequence of events.

Yet, somewhere deep within the bustling city of Pune, the invisible
threads of destiny had been quietly and drastically shifted. Because of
one man\'s silent intervention, several oblivious workers would return
home safely to their families that night, completely unaware of just how
terrifyingly close they had come to tragedy. And Kusha infinitely
preferred it to remain exactly that way. He sought absolutely no public
recognition, offered no elaborate explanations, and ensured his profound
secrets remained securely hidden. It was just another deadly storm that
he had clearly seen coming from miles away, allowing him to quietly
guide another group of innocent people safely out of its destructive
path.
