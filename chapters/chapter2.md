**Chapter 2: The Past -- Part 1**

The very first time Prajwala noticed Kusha, he wasn\'t doing anything
remotely remarkable. He was simply sitting in the cool shade beneath a
sprawling rain tree near the Mechanical Engineering block of the
Maharashtra Institute of Technology in Pune. A closed notebook rested
casually on his lap, and a half-finished cup of tea sat forgotten on the
bench beside him. As the new academic year officially began, students
hurried past him in every conceivable direction, yet he didn\'t seem the
least bit interested in the chaotic rush. He wasn\'t reading, he wasn\'t
scrolling through his phone, and he certainly wasn\'t trying to network.
He was simply, quietly watching the world.

For a reason she couldn\'t quite pinpoint, his stillness caught her
attention. While the vast majority of freshmen spent their entire first
week aggressively trying to be noticed and form alliances, Kusha looked
perfectly comfortable being entirely ignored. Because Prajwala was the
sort of person who preferred to carefully evaluate people before forming
opinions, she naturally slowed her pace slightly as she walked past his
bench.

Their eyes met for barely a fraction of a second. He offered a polite,
unremarkable nod.

\"Morning,\" he murmured, his voice barely carrying over the campus
noise.

\"Morning,\" she replied reflexively, continuing on her way.

It was a completely ordinary exchange, yet for the remainder of that
afternoon, she found herself inexplicably returning to the image of the
quiet boy under the tree.

The first semester rushed by in a blur of assignments, demanding
workshops, stressful internal examinations, and endless lab records.
During this time, Prajwala and Kusha weren\'t exactly friends. They
occupied the same crowded classrooms and attended the same monotonous
lectures, occasionally exchanging notes or sitting near one another
during practicals. Every interaction, however, left Prajwala with the
exact same impression: Kusha listened far more than he ever spoke.

The atmosphere between them---and around him---shifted drastically one
tense afternoon after a particularly grueling mechanics lecture.
Prajwala was lingering in the corridor, listening as a group of students
anxiously discussed the impending semester results. Everyone was trying
to mask their anxiety, but a student named Ramesh was spiraling.

\"I\'m dead,\" Ramesh groaned, running a trembling hand through his
hair. \"If I fail this examination, my father is absolutely going to end
me. I mean it. I\'m completely dead.\"

Quietly closing his notebook, Kusha looked up from his spot against the
wall. \"Your father won\'t kill you, Ramesh.\"

Ramesh whipped around, glaring defensively. \"You don\'t know my father,
Kusha! The man measures my worth in percentages!\"

\"I don\'t know him, no,\" Kusha agreed, his voice a calm anchor in the
chaotic hallway. \"But I know you\'ll survive whatever happens
tomorrow.\"

Then, Kusha's expression hardened into something unusually serious. The
sudden shift in his normally placid tone silenced the entire group. He
stepped closer, looking directly into Ramesh\'s panicked eyes.

\"Listen to me,\" Kusha said, his voice dropping a register. \"Whatever
the result is tomorrow, don\'t do anything impulsive. Promise me.\"

Ramesh blinked, entirely derailed by the intense, bizarre demand.
\"What? Why are you being so weird about this?\"

\"Just promise me, Ramesh.\"

\"Fine,\" Ramesh muttered, shifting uncomfortably. \"I promise. God.\"

Satisfied, Kusha gave a curt nod, adjusted his backpack, and walked
away. While the rest of the students quickly brushed off the exchange as
Kusha just being eccentric, Prajwala felt a sudden, inexplicable chill
run down her spine.

When the results were published the next morning, Ramesh had failed two
vital subjects.

The devastating news shattered him. For several agonizing hours, nobody
on campus knew where he had disappeared to. By the time he returned that
evening, he was pale, silent, and visibly changed. It was only later
that the terrifying truth leaked out: Ramesh had wandered off to a
secluded area far outside the city limits, standing on a literal
precipice as he spiraled into dark thoughts of failure and the permanent
urge to end everything.

The following day, an emotional Ramesh tracked Kusha down near the
college canteen. Without a word of warning, he grabbed Kusha by the
shoulders, pulling him into a desperate, trembling embrace.

\"You knew,\" Ramesh choked out, pulling back to stare at him, tears
welling in his eyes. \"How did you know I was going to fail?\"

Kusha offered a faint, enigmatic smile, gently prying Ramesh\'s hands
off his shoulders. \"Intuition, Ramesh. That\'s all it was. Anyone could
see how stressed you were.\"

\"No!\" Ramesh fiercely shook his head, his voice cracking. \"I was
standing there, Kusha. I was ready to jump. And then your voice\... your
stupid warning just echoed in my head. *Don\'t do anything impulsive.*
You saved my life.\"

The air between them grew incredibly heavy. Kusha placed a gentle,
grounding hand on Ramesh\'s shoulder. \"Never consider giving up your
life again,\" he instructed softly. \"No exam is worth it.\"

For the rest of the campus, the drama ended right there. For Prajwala,
who watched the entire encounter from a nearby table, it was the very
first piece of an intricate puzzle she was determined to solve.

By the time the third semester rolled around, their paths crossed far
more frequently. They frequently found themselves sitting near one
another during long lectures or sharing quiet tea breaks. None of these
interactions were intentional; they simply happened with an easy,
natural rhythm. Yet, the mystery surrounding him only deepened, peaking
during a disastrous Manufacturing Processes laboratory.

The professor in charge was brilliant but famously temperamental. That
dreary afternoon, he stormed into the laboratory in a visibly foul mood.

\"You think this is a joke?!\" the professor bellowed, slamming a heavy
textbook onto a workbench, making several students flinch. \"You come in
here with half-finished records and expect to pass? Tomorrow, I will
show you exactly what a difficult practical examination looks like!
I\'ll fail every last one of you!\"

He stormed out of the room, leaving behind an eruption of sheer panic.
As anxious students huddled together, lamenting their inevitable doom,
Kusha remained perfectly undisturbed. He calmly packed away his tools
and neatly closed his record book.

\"How are you so calm?\" a bewildered classmate demanded, gesturing
wildly at the door. \"He\'s going to butcher our GPAs tomorrow!\"

Kusha offered a gentle smile. \"Worrying won\'t miraculously improve
your marks. Just go home, study properly, and come back tomorrow.\"

Unwilling to let the moment pass, Prajwala actively sought him out,
eventually finding him in the parking area as he unlocked his
motorcycle.

\"Alright, out with it,\" she said, marching up to him with her arms
firmly crossed.

Kusha paused, helmet in hand, and raised an eyebrow. \"Out with what?\"

\"Don\'t play dumb, Kusha,\" she challenged, stepping closer. \"What do
you know about tomorrow\'s exam? You\'re too calm.\"

He offered an irritatingly evasive smile. \"I know that it\'s worth
fifty marks and starts at nine in the morning.\"

\"Stop deflecting.\"

He looked at her for a moment, the amusement fading into that same
unshakable certainty he had shown with Ramesh. \"The examination will
happen, Prajwala. But he won\'t be there to conduct it. Good luck
studying.\"

Before a stunned Prajwala could demand a proper explanation, he strapped
on his helmet and drove away.

The next morning, his unbelievable prediction came true. The dreaded
professor was entirely absent, replaced by a remarkably pleasant
instructor. Following the session, the lab in-charge casually mentioned
that the angry professor had been unexpectedly admitted to the hospital
overnight with a severe kidney stone.

While the rest of the class buzzed with excited gossip, Prajwala
remained completely silent, her eyes fixed solely on Kusha. He was
quietly arranging his papers, acting as if he hadn\'t accurately
predicted a medical emergency less than twenty-four hours prior.

*How on earth does he know?*

As they transitioned into their fourth semester, their quiet
companionship blossomed into a friendship that was impossible to ignore.
They spent hours discussing intricate projects and arguing over trivial
things. Naturally, their friend groups noticed.

One vibrant afternoon, as Prajwala approached a table in the crowded
canteen carrying her lunch tray, her three closest friends immediately
exchanged mischievous smiles.

\"Looking for someone, Prajwala?\" her friend Sneha teased, leaning
forward. \"Maybe someone who likes to sit under rain trees?\"

\"Shut up, Sneha,\" Prajwala snapped, a furious blush spreading across
her cheeks as she set her tray down. \"I\'m just looking for a place to
sit.\"

\"Sure you are,\" another friend laughed. \"That\'s why you scanned the
entire left wing of the canteen first.\"

The situation worsened significantly when Kusha walked through the
canteen entrance moments later. He approached their table, effortlessly
navigating the crowded room.

\"What\'s the joke?\" Kusha asked politely, pulling up an empty chair.
\"Mind if I join?\"

\"Nothing!\" Prajwala blurted out quickly, glaring at her giggling
friends. \"Just\... stupid engineering humor. Have a seat.\"

Kusha, utilizing his signature patience, merely smiled and chose not to
press the issue.

As the weeks turned into months, Prajwala wrestled with a difficult
internal contradiction. Every strange, predictive incident dramatically
increased her suspicions regarding his true nature. Yet, paradoxically,
every normal, mundane conversation they shared deepened her profound
trust in him. She was growing increasingly certain that he possessed
knowledge he had no earthly business having, but she never once felt
unsafe or manipulated in his presence.

One quiet evening, as they strolled out of the sprawling campus together
under a fading sky, Prajwala turned to look at him. The air was cool,
the campus finally emptying out.

\"Kusha?\" she asked tentatively. \"Can I ask you a question?\"

He looked over at her, his dark eyes catching the orange glow of the
streetlights. A faint smile touched his lips. \"You already just did.\"

She rolled her eyes slightly but didn\'t laugh. For several long,
agonizing seconds, she seriously considered finally dropping the facade.
She wanted to ask the heavy question that had been haunting her for
months: *How do you know these things? What are you hiding?*

She opened her mouth, but her quiet courage faltered. The difficult
words refused to come.

\"Nothing,\" she murmured, shaking her head and looking down at the
pavement. \"Never mind.\"

Kusha stopped walking and looked at her for a long, piercing moment. His
deep, observant gaze made her genuinely wonder if he already knew
exactly what she had wanted to ask. Finally, the tension broke.

\"Alright,\" he said softly, giving a slow, understanding nod.

They continued their walk in silence, both lost in their own thoughts,
both quietly waiting, and neither truly ready for the monumental
conversation that would eventually change everything between them.
