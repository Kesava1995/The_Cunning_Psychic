**Chapter 4: The Past -- Part 2**

The golden summer sun hung low over the sprawling fields, casting long,
dramatic shadows across the vibrant landscape. Prajwala strolled
thoughtfully along the narrow mud path that cut directly through her
family\'s ancestral farmland. A refreshing, brief afternoon shower had
recently passed, leaving behind the rich, comforting scent of wet soil
that lingered pleasantly in the humid air. To her left, a neat row of
lush mango trees lined the edge of the path, while to her right, vibrant
green crops stretched out endlessly toward the distant horizon.

Her parents had departed earlier that morning to spend the entire day
visiting nearby relatives, leaving her completely alone on the property
for the very first time since their arrival in the village.
Unfortunately for Prajwala, being entirely alone meant she had far too
much quiet time for thinking. She found herself thinking extensively
about college, pondering the heavy academic demands of their impending
final year, and, most prominently, thinking about a remarkably calm
mechanical engineering student who somehow managed to occupy her
thoughts far more often than she was ever willing to admit.

A small, involuntary smile graced her features as Kusha crossed her
mind. Letting out a soft, wistful sigh, she murmured to the empty fields
that just one meeting with him during this long, tedious break would
have been incredibly nice.

A gentle breeze rustled through the tall crops, temporarily breaking the
silence of the rural afternoon. Moments later, the distinct sound of
approaching footsteps echoed on the path directly behind her. Prajwala
turned casually to see who was approaching, only to freeze entirely in
her tracks. For a fleeting, disorienting second, she genuinely wondered
if her persistent thoughts had caused her mind to completely fabricate
the image before her.

Standing on the mud path, with his hands comfortably tucked into his
pockets and looking exactly as unshakeably calm as he always did back in
Pune, was Kusha.

Prajwala blinked rapidly, desperately trying to process the
impossibility of the situation.

\"Kusha?\" she breathed, her voice laced with sheer disbelief.

\"Hello,\" he replied simply, offering a gentle smile that immediately
confirmed he was, in fact, entirely real.

Her heart immediately abandoned all rational attempts at maintaining a
normal rhythm, hammering wildly against her ribs. \"What on earth are
you doing here?\"

He casually glanced around at the sprawling green fields surrounding
them. \"Walking.\"

She stared at him, utterly bewildered by the mundane response.
\"Walking? In my obscure, remote village?\"

\"Apparently so,\" Kusha answered smoothly.

Prajwala folded her arms defensively across her chest, trying to project
an air of authority she did not feel. \"You know, Kusha, most normal
people only travel to remote villages because they actually have
relatives living there.\"

\"That certainly sounds like a reasonable assumption,\" he agreed
mildly.

\"And you?\" she pressed, refusing to let him deflect.

\"I was bored,\" he stated with absolute sincerity.

She narrowed her eyes, studying his tranquil expression for any hint of
deception. \"You are trying to tell me that you travelled all the way
out here simply because you were bored?\"

When Kusha offered a simple, confirming nod, Prajwala could not help but
burst into genuine laughter. The explanation was utterly ridiculous, yet
somehow, she found herself believing him entirely. Or perhaps, she
admitted quietly to herself, she simply did not care about the logic
behind it. The only thing that truly mattered in that moment was that he
was actually there.

\"Come on,\" she said, her smile widening.

\"Where are we going?\" Kusha asked.

\"I am going to show you the farm,\" she decided enthusiastically.

They spent the next hour walking comfortably side by side through the
vibrant fields. Prajwala happily pointed out everything of significance,
sharing the history of the lush mango orchard, explaining the mechanics
of the irrigation canal, and showing him the small, weathered storage
shed. She even led him to the old, stone well that her grandfather had
painstakingly built decades ago. Throughout the impromptu tour, Kusha
listened with his signature patience, occasionally asking thoughtful
questions and nodding in understanding, but mostly, he spent his time
quietly observing his surroundings.

Prajwala, however, realized that she was spending significantly more
time observing Kusha than she was actually looking at the farm. Every
few minutes, she found herself glancing sideways to admire his perfectly
calm expression and his quiet, unwavering focus. She was deeply moved by
the way he seemed genuinely, profoundly interested in every minor detail
she shared with him. She caught herself staring a bit too long several
times, quickly averting her gaze each time she realized what she was
doing. Fortunately, Kusha either pretended not to notice her lingering
looks, or he genuinely missed them. Prajwala nervously concluded that
both possibilities were equally dangerous to her heavily guarded heart.

\"You really love this place, don\'t you?\" Kusha remarked, breaking a
comfortable silence.

\"I really do,\" she admitted softly.

\"Why is that?\" he asked, turning his deep, observant gaze toward her.

She looked out across the sweeping fields, taking in the familiar,
comforting sight. \"Because everything here feels so incredibly simple
and uncomplicated.\"

Kusha nodded slowly, processing her words. \"That is a very rare
thing.\"

\"What is?\" Prajwala asked, confused.

\"Finding places that manage to stay entirely honest,\" he clarified
gently.

Prajwala smiled to herself, recognizing that this was exactly the kind
of strange, deeply philosophical answer that only Kusha could casually
deliver. And, as always, it somehow made perfect sense to her.

By the time evening began to fall, bathing the sky in brilliant strokes
of fiery orange, they finally reached the designated village bus stop. A
heavy, rattling bus was already approaching in the distance. A sudden,
intense wish washed over Prajwala, desperately hoping that the vehicle
would simply disappear so he could stay longer. Unfortunately, reality
prevailed, and the bus screeched to a halt before them. Kusha stepped up
onto the first metal stair, then paused and turned back to face her.

\"Take care of yourself, Prajwala,\" he said warmly.

\"You take care too,\" she replied, her voice carrying a hint of
reluctance.

He offered a final nod before the heavy folding doors hissed closed, and
a moment later, the bus pulled away, kicking up a cloud of dry dust.
Prajwala remained securely rooted to the spot, watching intently as the
vehicle became smaller and smaller, eventually fading into a distant,
indistinct shape before vanishing completely over the horizon. Only
after he was truly gone did she finally turn around and begin the long
walk back home, carrying a radiant smile that she simply could not hide.

Several months later, the grueling final year examinations had
officially concluded. Across Pune, relieved students celebrated their
freedom, proud families celebrated the culmination of years of hard
work, and seemingly everyone on campus was caught up in the joyous
atmosphere. Everyone, that is, except for Kusha.

While his peers were out enjoying their newly found liberty, Kusha was
confined to his bed, battling a miserable fever that had spiked to a
concerning one hundred and four degrees.

His mother, Kavya, hovered anxiously over him, carefully placing a
freshly soaked, cool handkerchief across his burning forehead. \"You
really need to rest, Kusha,\" she scolded gently.

\"I am resting, Mom,\" he croaked, his throat incredibly dry.

\"You were literally just reading a book,\" she pointed out
disapprovingly.

\"Reading while lying completely flat on a bed is a form of resting,\"
he argued weakly.

\"It absolutely is not,\" she countered strictly.

\"It certainly feels like resting to me,\" he sighed.

Kavya let out a heavy sigh of her own. The family doctor had already
visited the apartment twice, and thankfully, the dangerous fever was
finally beginning to reduce. However, the lingering weakness, the deep,
aching body pain, and the horribly unpleasant metallic taste in his
mouth remained firmly in place. Most importantly, and perhaps most
exhaustingly for Kusha, his mother\'s relentless, overflowing worry
remained entirely undiminished.

By the mid-afternoon, several of his close college friends arrived to
check on his recovery. The previously quiet bedroom quickly transformed
into a chaotic, noisy gathering space. Wild stories from their recent
celebrations emerged, highly embarrassing memories from their early
semesters resurfaced, and their classic, never-ending arguments about
their most difficult professors aggressively resumed. Everybody laughed
loudly, and for two solid hours, the space felt significantly less like
a dreary sick room and much more like a lively college classroom.

Eventually, the energetic group realized their friend needed peace,
promising to meet up again soon before shuffling out of the apartment.
The house finally settled back into its comfortable, quiet state. Kusha
gratefully closed his heavy eyes, preparing to sleep.

Just a few minutes later, however, he heard another distinct set of
footsteps approaching down the hallway. These steps were much gentler,
far more hesitant, and significantly slower than his boisterous friends.
Without needing to open his eyes, Kusha already knew exactly who was
standing outside his door.

The wooden door creaked open, and Prajwala quietly stepped into the
room. For a long, stretched moment, neither of them spoke a single word.
Then, as her eyes adjusted to the dim lighting, she finally saw him
properly. The pale, sickly complexion of his face, the dark circles of
exhaustion under his eyes, and the undeniable physical weakness
radiating from him hit her like a physical blow. Something incredibly
heavy tightened painfully inside her chest, and her eyes immediately
welled up with unshed tears. Panicking, she quickly turned her face
away, desperately hoping to hide her emotional reaction before he could
notice.

Unfortunately for Prajwala, she was temporarily forgetting one crucially
important fact about the man she was visiting: Kusha noticed absolutely
everything.

She walked over and carefully sat in the wooden chair positioned beside
his bed. A thick, emotionally charged silence settled deeply between
them. Unlike most silences, neither of them seemed particularly
uncomfortable with it, and neither seemed terribly eager to break the
quiet tension.

Finally, clearing her throat softly, she spoke. \"You really scared
everyone, you know.\"

\"I certainly wasn\'t trying to,\" Kusha replied, his voice barely above
a rasp.

\"You looked significantly worse last week,\" she noted, her voice
trembling slightly.

\"So I have been repeatedly told,\" he agreed with a small, weary smile.

Prajwala managed to return a faint, shaky smile. Believing he was not
looking directly at her, she quickly reached up and wiped the rogue
tears from her eyes. Kusha, however, slowly turned his heavy head and
looked straight at her. His gaze held absolutely no surprise or
confusion regarding her sudden tears. Instead, his dark eyes held only
profound, bottomless understanding---the exact same, unnerving level of
understanding that had always managed to simultaneously comfort and
deeply unsettle her. It was the same inexplicable understanding that
often felt entirely impossible for a normal human being to possess.

Without saying a single word to bridge the gap, Kusha slowly reached out
from under his blankets and gently placed his remarkably warm hand
securely over hers. It was a remarkably simple gesture, containing
nothing more than quiet reassurance, yet the emotional effect on
Prajwala was immediate and overwhelming.

She froze entirely, her breath catching sharply in her throat. Because
in that incredibly vulnerable, silent moment, a massive realization
slammed into her. No, it was not just a single realization; it was a
cascade of several devastating truths snapping violently into place.

He knew. He had absolutely always known. He knew about her deeply hidden
romantic feelings. He knew about her quiet, internal worries. He knew
about her unspoken fears. He knew absolutely everything she had tried so
desperately to conceal.

And then, a sudden, terrifying secondary realization rapidly surfaced
from the depths of her memory. The village. Her remote, ancestral
village. She had never, not even once, mentioned its exact location to a
single person at their college. She had never told her closest
classmates, she had never confided in her immediate friend group, and
she had certainly never shared those details with the people she spoke
with on a regular basis.

Yet, Kusha had flawlessly appeared there, walking down her private dirt
path as if he already knew exactly where he was going. As if he had
always possessed that knowledge.

The scattered, confusing pieces of their entire history suddenly
connected with terrifying clarity. Every incredibly strange incident
they had witnessed. Every impossible, statistical anomaly of a
coincidence. Every perfectly timed warning he had issued. Every single
accurate prediction. Every thoughtful answer he had provided before the
actual question was even fully articulated. The massive, impossible
conclusion that she had spent years actively avoiding finally stood
undeniable before her.

Kusha wasn\'t just exceptionally lucky. Kusha wasn\'t merely making
highly educated guesses. Kusha wasn\'t imagining things or possessing
strong intuition. He truly, factually knew things that he had absolutely
no earthly, logical way of knowing.

A tidal wave of pure, unadulterated panic hit her all at once. Her
breathing quickened erratically as an intense mixture of fear, deep
confusion, strange relief, overwhelming love, and profound shock
collided violently within her mind. She stood up so abruptly that her
wooden chair loudly scraped against the floor.

\"Kusha---\" she began, but her voice choked. Absolutely no coherent
words could follow.

Fresh, uncontrollable tears fully escaped her eyes before she could even
attempt to stop them. Completely overwhelmed by the magnitude of her
discovery, she sharply turned on her heel and rushed desperately toward
the bedroom door.

\"Prajwala---\" Kusha called out softly, a rare hint of regret lacing
his tired voice.

She didn\'t stop. Seconds later, she was entirely gone, leaving behind a
heavy, vibrating silence that filled the sick room.

Kusha stared blankly at the empty doorway for a long, agonizing minute
before letting out a defeated sigh. \"I really shouldn\'t have done
that,\" he muttered to himself.

A few moments later, drawn by the sudden commotion of Prajwala\'s hasty
exit, his parents slowly entered the bedroom. Kavya looked deeply
concerned as she surveyed her son\'s weary face.

\"What on earth just happened?\" she asked worriedly.

Kusha carefully leaned back against his propped-up pillows, physically
exhausted from the brief exchange. \"Nothing bad,\" he promised quietly.

Sanjay raised a highly skeptical eyebrow, crossing his arms over his
chest. \"Her running out of here in tears certainly didn\'t look like
nothing,\" he observed astutely.

Kusha remained completely quiet for several long seconds, carefully
processing his next words. Then, he spoke with remarkable softness.
\"She has feelings for me.\"

Kavya blinked in sheer surprise. \"Oh.\"

Sanjay also blinked, his stern expression softening slightly. \"Oh.\"

\"She has never actually told anyone about them,\" Kusha continued, his
gaze still fixed toward the door.

Kavya slowly sat down on the edge of the mattress, trying to understand
the situation. \"And now?\"

Kusha looked specifically toward the empty space where Prajwala had just
disappeared, the weight of his reality pressing down heavily upon him.
\"And now, she knows that I know,\" he answered cryptically.

Neither of his parents spoke another word. Because, living with him for
over two decades, all three of them implicitly understood that there was
another, far more massive truth hidden securely beneath those simple
words. Prajwala hadn\'t just unexpectedly discovered her exposed
romantic feelings. She had finally, definitively discovered his ultimate
secret.

And for the very first time in their incredibly close relationship, that
profound realization had truly frightened her.

Kusha closed his eyes tightly. It was not because the intense fever was
returning, but because he intimately understood the tragic burden of his
gifts---he knew that some profound truths inevitably arrived long before
people were emotionally ready to handle them.
